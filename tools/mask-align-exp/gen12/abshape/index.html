abshape A/B — OUTLINE vs SOLID FILLED guide shapes

Experiment: gen12 templated blueprint. Identical structural prompt across conditions (4-substitution wording diff for B, recorded in each results.json). Two rounds across contrasting themes, 2 seeds each (121 & 134), n=2/cell.
Model: gemini-3-pro-image-preview via Vertex AI (project muser-2605300220, global endpoint), 4K, 5:4 — same model the fal path proxies. Generation goes through genskin_ab.py's Vertex-direct edit_vertex() (gcloud access-token auth), NOT fal, so both rounds ran independent of any fal account status. Scoring: genskin leak gate + extract12 pass 1 (no matte).
Cost: 8 gens × ~$0.24/4K image ≈ $1.92 total (round 1 fa-pod: 4 × $0.24 ≈ $0.96; round 2 wc-goldshield: 4 × $0.24 ≈ $0.96; extraction/scoring local, $0).
Verdict:
SOLID FILLED guides (B) beat OUTLINE guides (A) — lean but consistent, across both themes tested. n=2 seeds x 2 themes = 4 gens/condition, so treat this as directional, not conclusive.

What's consistent: the automated leak-% gate (worst-control key-colour bleed) does NOT discriminate — A avg ~0.14%, B avg ~0.14%, both fail it on 2/4 gens each. But that metric only samples a narrow per-control interior and MISSES a defect visible on every A gen at full-res: a thin RING/bezel painted in the exact guide hex colour, wrapped around sockets (vol/seek/shuffle) and, on wc-goldshield, around every transport BUTTON too (magenta ring on playpause, green on prev, pink on next, red on queue in wc-goldshield-a-121; matching aqua ring on the vol socket). That ring is the literal un-erased alignment marking the prompt explicitly bans ("if a button has a coloured ring, it is WRONG"). B's guide-colour bleed-through, when it happens, gets absorbed into the design as a coherent element instead (a candy-coloured whole-button fill in fa-pod, a gem-inlay medallion in wc-goldshield-b-121) rather than reading as a stray unfinished ring - a materially less broken failure mode even though both are technically 'guide colour survived'.

The one gate that DOES discriminate cleanly: emptiness (are the knob/seek/shuffle cavities actually empty). Round 1 (fa-pod) was a wash (A: 1/2 pass, B: 1/2 pass). Round 2 (wc-goldshield, a dark gold/blue heraldic theme, chosen to check whether round 1's read was fa-pod-specific) was NOT a wash: A failed emptiness 2/2 (both seeds baked a solid violet-glass fill into the shuffle slot, 10.2% and 23.4% bright interior vs a <1% clean-empty baseline) while B passed emptiness 2/2 (shuffle 0.0% both seeds, clean dark gear-toothed cavity). Combined emptiness score across both themes: A 1/4, B 3/4.

Combined read: B is the better default. Not because its numeric leak% is lower (it isn't, reliably) but because (a) it wins emptiness 3:1 pooled across both themes, (b) it never produces the tell-tale thin ring-around-a-button defect that A produces in 3 of 4 A gens, and (c) round 2 confirms round 1's directional lean rather than contradicting it. Caveats: gate failures happen on both sides for OTHER reasons unrelated to guide style (b-121/fa-pod had a mis-registered/undetected shuffle region; b-121/wc-goldshield failed on 'region-misplaced:album_art') so B is not defect-free, just fails on placement/registration rather than colour-residue. Recommend: adopt SOLID guides as the templated-blueprint default; separately, extract12's leak gate should sample button perimeters too (out of scope here — extract12.py is shared pipeline, not touched by this experiment) since it currently misses a defect obvious to the eye.
