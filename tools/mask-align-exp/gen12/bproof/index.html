B-proof: does gen12's constraint load degrade paint quality?

One variable changed at a time: the prompt. Same base model, matched seeds, same theme_prompt text verbatim, flat pale backdrop. Round 1 (6 themes) is a binary light-vs-heavy comparison; round 2 (3 themes) adds a middle tier to test whether quality falls off gradually or at a threshold.

What you're deciding

Adopt the B-pivot — beautiful-render-first, detect-second — or keep the current constrained, pipeline-first single-pass generation?

  • Keep current (constrained single-pass): gen12's ~9-11k-char prompt guarantees empty knob/seek/ toggle cavities, exact-fit sprite-strip parts, and a pixel-aligned region mask in ONE generation — the runtime seats real controls with zero detection risk. Cost: visibly flatter, less "photographed" paint quality (see below), even on regions the prompt doesn't constrain.
  • Adopt B-pivot: paint with a short, unconstrained prompt (what "froggo-style" approximates here) to get the richer look, then RECOVER control geometry from the finished art via VLM/SAM/detector. Cost: gives up the guaranteed-empty-cavity / exact-fit correctness that a detector has to re-earn (and per ai-image-coords-rule / discover-before-building-rule, detector-based placement has burned real time before on this project).

Evidence below: round 1 shows the quality gap holds across 6 visually diverse themes (not just the original 2). Round 2 shows WHERE the gap opens — gradually, or in one jump.

Round 2 — 3-tier prompt-load ramp (linear vs cliff)

Round 1 only has 2 points on the constraint-length curve (light, heavy) — that can't tell a smooth decline from a threshold effect. Round 2 adds a MEDIUM tier and holds the input/output FORMAT constant across all 3 tiers (unlike round 1's heavy condition, which was the real two-column pipeline artifact) so prompt length is the only variable. Themes: steam-porthole & diablo-gothic at FRESH seeds (284, 310 — different from round 1's 84, 110) + wmp-quicksilver (not yet used elsewhere in this lookbook) at seed 405. Tiers are literal layered subsets of genskin.py's real clauses (not paraphrased) — light ~600-640 chars, medium ~2,060-2,100 chars (+ empty-cavity + blank-screens + seek-slot-only rules), heavy ~3,555-3,590 chars (+ embossed-button-relief with the real per-icon roster + full no-text rule + a closing reinforcement paragraph). All via Vertex AI, same serving path as round 1's froggo-style renders.

Confounds we could NOT fully control (read before concluding)

  • Serving path (round 1): gen12 rolls went through fal's proxy of the model; froggo-style rolls went through Vertex AI directly (fal's account was locked for most of this session). Same published base model (gemini-3-pro-image-preview), same seed integer passed — but seed→RNG mapping across the two serving stacks is not proven identical, so "same seed" is nominal, not bit-exact.
  • Canvas area per device (round 1): gen12's device shares a 5:4 4K canvas with the mask panel and sprite strip (device ~2304x2784px ~6.4MP); the froggo-style device gets the whole 4:5 4K canvas (~17MP, most of which is the device). The light condition has roughly 2x the pixel budget on the device itself.
  • Input image differs by construction (round 1): blueprint/scaffold vs flat canvas. Part of the "constraint load" variable by design, but it means layout freedom differs too.
  • Frozen snapshot (round 1, steam-porthole & diablo-gothic only): the gen12 "heavy" images shown were captured 2026-07-09 ~10:00 against the pipeline's THEN-passing seeds (84, 110). The live assets-* dirs for both themes were regenerated later the same day by unrelated pipeline hardening work (now at seeds 623, 710) — this page intentionally keeps the original frozen comparison rather than re-cutting crops from a moving target.
  • Round 2's "heavy" tier is NOT the literal shipping prompt. It's a same-format (single flat canvas) superset of real clauses reaching ~3.6k chars — not the two-column blueprint+mask ~9-11k-char prompt, because that prompt asks for a structurally different task (painting a second mask column), which would reintroduce round 1's format confound instead of isolating prompt length.
  • The froggo / round-2 renders are NOT pipeline-usable at the light tier: screens have baked content, knob/thumb/toggle are installed, no mask panel, no sprite strip. Medium and heavy tiers DO ask for empty cavities/blank screens (that's the point) but still produce no mask panel or sprite strip.

    • Assessor's read (visual, all renders inspected at full res)

  • Round 1 generalizes past the original 2 themes. The light-prompt render is visibly richer than its matched gen12 render on every one of the 6 themes — real material variation (patina, wear, specular breakup), more volumetric lighting, higher perceived production value, and the gap holds even on surfaces both conditions render freely (button facets, body panels). gen12's paint reads flatter, more "clean illustration"; the light-prompt paint reads as photographed.
  • Round 2's honest finding: the drop is NOT a smooth ramp, and it front-loads. Across all 3 round-2 themes, the biggest visible quality loss happens between LIGHT and MEDIUM — the moment the prompt requires hollow/empty sockets and blank screens instead of a fully "alive" device. MEDIUM→HEAVY (adding button-relief prose, the full no-text rule, and a reinforcement paragraph) does NOT reliably cost further quality: diablo-gothic keeps declining slightly, wmp-quicksilver is roughly flat, and steam-porthole's heavy tier is arguably MORE ornate than its medium tier (glowing lenses/tubes, a fuller round housing).
  • Read with caution: this is 3 themes x 1 seed each — not enough to rule out per-seed variance driving the steam-porthole non-monotonicity. But the consistent pattern (light→medium is the big drop, medium→heavy is small/flat/reversed) points at a specific candidate mechanism: it may be the EMPTY- CAVITY / BLANK-SCREEN requirement specifically — not sheer prompt length — that costs quality, since round 2's heavy tier adds length without adding new "make it look unfinished" constraints. If that holds up, it reframes the B-pivot question: the real cost driver may not be constraint LOAD in general but this ONE class of constraint (forcing a "before assembly" incomplete look), which a beautiful-render-first approach would also have to solve some other way (e.g. composite the empty cavity onto a fully-painted device rather than asking the model to paint it in from the start).
    • Decided — 2026-07-11. Heavy prompt ACCEPTED, no B-pivot. The measured quality cost is accepted as the price of reliable empty sockets / runtime detectability; gen12's heavy structural-constraint prompt stays the production architecture.
