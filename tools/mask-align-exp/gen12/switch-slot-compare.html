Method notes

  • All three variants render the exact SAME lever sprite (switchslot-compare/lever-sprite.png, cut from the shapematch regen's strip) — the only variable across cards is HOW the housing was produced. This was a deliberate substitution: the mainline baseline's own lever cut turned out to be a plain rounded-rect at the alpha-silhouette level (its "grip end" was surface shading only, not a true silhouette bump — column-height profiling showed <3px variation across 90% of its width) — dilating it would have just produced another pill. The shapematch lever has a genuine dogbone/keyhole silhouette (height dips ~44% at the waist), so it drives A and B too, keeping the comparison meaningful.
  • A and B share the same starting backdrop: assets-fallout-pipboy-tt-baseline (a fresh mainline TOGGLE_TRACK_ENABLED regen — pill housing + separately-cut fun lever, i.e. the CURRENT default architecture and exactly the mismatch this page is about) with its baked pill track erased via erase12.erase_classical (OpenCV Telea inpaint, $0, visible as a soft blur under variant A where the CSS housing doesn't fully cover it).
  • C is a full independent regen with genskin.py's toggle clauses edited to require the housing and lever share one silhouette family — see the diff below. That edit is UNCOMMITTED and flagged off by default.

CONCLUSION / VERDICT

VERDICT: C (baked, ~$0.24/regen) — RUNNER-UP: B (inpainted, ~$0.04/edit). Reviewed by google/gemini-2.5-pro via openrouter/router/vision (~$0.034/call) on close-up ON/OFF crops of all three, cross-checked against direct pixel inspection per verify-rule (the VLM is a witness, not the judge, on shape geometry).

C — material/lighting are 100% consistent because the housing was generated in the SAME paint call as the rest of the device (scratches, edge wear, rim bevel all match). Best visual result, no artifacts, but needed a manual pixel-measured position correction on this page: extract12.py's track-walk (tuned for plain channels) under- captured this dogbone housing's true width by ~13% and mis-centred it — a real, generalizable finding: shipping this direction means also widening the walk's shape assumptions, not just the prompt.

B — genuinely strong for the price: the AI-inpainted recess reads as a real carved dogbone socket with plausible depth and rim, correctly occludes the hazard stripe behind it, and needed no manual position correction (it landed where the erased pill used to be). Best cost/quality tradeoff of the three.

A — the VLM overstated one claim (said the housing's silhouette "reads as a single indistinct blob" with the waist "completely lost" — false on direct inspection, the dogbone waist IS visible in the render, see the mask preview and the live page). The REAL weakness, confirmed by looking directly: the recess reads as a flat CSS gradient with no metal texture, and the erase-classical cleanup underneath left a visible soft blur that isn't fully hidden by the rim. $0 and deterministic, but not shippable as-is without a better recess material treatment (e.g. sample real nearby body texture into the mask instead of a flat gradient) and a less lossy erase pass.

Recommendation: if per-skin spend is acceptable, bake it (C) and fix the track-walk width assumption in extract12.py so it generalizes past pill/channel shapes (per fix-generalizable-rule — a pipeline fix, not a per-skin patch). If spend must stay near-zero, B is the pragmatic middle: real generative quality at 6x less than a full regen, reusable on any already-generated skin's existing paint. A is only worth revisiting with a textured (not flat-gradient) recess material.
