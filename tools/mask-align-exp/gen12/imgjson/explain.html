Can the paint model hand back its own coordinates?

Dev-facing walkthrough of a completed eval — this page narrates index.html's raw results for a reader who wasn't in the room. Every image, JSON snippet, and number below is pulled directly from this experiment's committed out/ and viz/ artifacts — nothing here is simulated.
Models: gemini-3-pro-image-preview (the paint model) + gemini-3.1-pro-preview (the pipeline's existing text/director model), both direct Vertex AI generateContent · Total spend: ≈$0.45 — see §6 for the breakdown.

1The question

When you hand gemini-3-pro-image-preview — the same model that paints gen12's skins — a picture and ask it to also report back where things are as JSON, does it give you usable coordinates? If so, the pipeline could get self-reported mask-cell manifests for free, or even skip the separate detection pass entirely. This page walks through what actually came back.

2The setup

One source image, read-only: gen12/assets-wc-goldshield/paint.png (2304×3712px) — a top-down, fantasy-shield-styled media player skin. It has exactly 10 controls, always present exactly once: 5 buttons, 1 knob, 1 slider, 1 toggle, and 2 flat regions.

Ground truth is assets-wc-goldshield/regions.json's regions[*].device boxes — the same boxes extract12.py's pixel-space detector already produced for this skin, normalized [x,y,w,h] against the paint's own pixel frame. Every arm below gets graded against these exact 10 boxes.

3The arms

Same paint, same 10-control roster, five different asks. Each card shows the (condensed) prompt sent, a REAL verbatim snippet of what came back, and the scored overlay.

Look at the playpause row. "w" appears TWICE and "h" never does — valid JSON (a repeated key just overwrites), but score_pred() does float(item["h"]), gets a KeyError, and silently drops the row. 4The broken-frame finding

Arm A's raw score (mean IoU 0.003) looks like noise — as if the model just guessed. It isn't. Look at the x coordinates alone: they land almost exactly on target (scale 0.999 against ground truth). It's specifically y that's off, and not randomly — every y comes back compressed toward the top by a consistent factor.

each control (10 pts) if the model reported y in the paint's own frame (y=x) what it actually does — gt = 0.7335·pred + 0.0649, RMS 38px

Every one of the 10 points sits on the amber line, not the dashed blue "same frame" line — that's a real, consistent affine relationship, not scatter. The model sees the layout correctly — the ORDER and relative spacing of the 10 controls in its reported y matches the real image — it just reports the number against some internal preprocessed view (likely a resized/padded canvas it operates on internally) instead of the pixel frame of the image you actually sent it. Undo that one factor (plus a visualizer/album_art label swap the model also made) and arm A's mean IoU jumps from 0.003 to 0.53. The catch: that undo requires ground truth to fit — in production you don't have ground truth, so the raw output stays unusable.

Arm C (the text model) does NOT have this bug — its raw y-coordinates land directly in the paint's own frame, no rescue needed. This frame-shift is specific to the image model, not a property of asking Gemini for coordinates in general.

6Verdicts

Image-model JSON output — dead for manifests and for detection. It only speaks TEXT when IMAGE is also in the modality list, structured-output params are a hard HTTP 400 on this model, every response opens with ~2.5k chars of thinking-narration prose despite "STRICT JSON ONLY", and the y-axis lands in an uncalibratable internal frame. None of this is fixable from the prompt side.

responseSchema — adopt it on the director and on extraction. Costs nothing in spatial quality, and it deletes exactly the failure modes director.ts currently hand-validates (missing fields, invalid enum values) — C's dropped-h bug above is precisely what a required schema field prevents by construction.

Structured JSON-shaped prompts (s4) — measured neutral, not worth adopting alone. 13.1px vs C's 13.2px is noise. The paint-generation side of this (does a fenced-JSON blueprint spec help image gen itself) was not tested this round — untested, not refuted.

extract12.py stays load-bearing. Even the best VLM arm here (~13px centers on unambiguous controls) still carries a 20–30px tail and a real semantic-swap hazard (vol/shuffle, visualizer/album_art) that pixel-space detection doesn't have. A plausible future use: a cheap name↔position sanity witness that flags when detection and a VLM disagree — not a replacement for the detector itself.

Cost: gemini-3-pro-image-preview (image model, $120/1M image-out tok, $2/1M in) — A $0.05, B $0.16 (incl. 1120 image-out tok for the re-render), 2 modality probes $0.04, ~3 debug rolls $0.15 · gemini-3.1-pro-preview (text model, $12/1M text-out tok) — C + 6×s1 + s2 + s4, all 10 calls together $0.04 · s3 (image model + schema) rejected with HTTP 400 before any tokens billed = $0 · total ≈ $0.45.
