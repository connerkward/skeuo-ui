Cutting sprites: one global BiRefNet + mask correlation  vs  mask correlation + per-part BiRefNet

Two ways to matte the sprite parts out of the raw paint. A: run BiRefNet once over the whole paint, then use the mask to correlate each matte island to a control. B: use the mask to locate each region first, then run BiRefNet per crop. Same 5 parts (vol · bal · seek · toggle-off · toggle-on), real fal generations, checkerboard = transparency.

− ~2.4× the cost and 5× the calls (more latency); relies on the mask bbox being roughly right (it's the guide).

Verdict: hybrid. Use A (one global pass) for the device body cutout — it's large, mattes fine at global resolution, and it's one cheap call; let the mask correlate identity. Use B (per-part) only for the small sprite parts, where the global downscale is too coarse and island bboxes clip. That's global-cut-the-device + per-part-cut-the-sprites, mask-correlated throughout — cheapest where quality doesn't matter, sharp where it does. Pure-A is fine if you accept softer small-part edges and pad the island bbox to stop clipping; pure-B is the safe default if edges matter and the ~2.4× cost is acceptable.
Both are strictly better than my earlier client-side luma key (a hard max<74 threshold) — BiRefNet gives a real anti-aliased matte. The mask stays the guide/correlator in every case, never the pixel-exact cut (it drifts and isn't tight — the through-line of this whole experiment).
