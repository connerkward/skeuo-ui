# Hooks Library

**How to use:** Pull a hook to bait a reader in — drop one verbatim (quotes kept exact, attribution intact) at the top of a post (OPENER), mid-flow to pivot (ASIDE/inject), atop a section to frame it (PREFACE), or at the end as the turn (CLOSER). Each entry carries the hook, a one-line gloss, and a "use it to…" tag. The same hook may appear in both the FUNCTION and THEME indexes — full text lives in the FUNCTION section; the THEME section cross-references by name. Paraphrases I tightened are marked *(tightened)*; everything in quotes is verbatim from the notes.

## Best of the box

The five sharpest — erudite, vivid, instantly usable.

1. **Etak (Micronesian navigation).** "A method of mental navigation in which the navigator envisions themselves as stationary, while islands and stars move." — A whole epistemology in one word: you don't move through the world, the world moves past you. *Use it to* open any piece on perspective, interfaces, or reframing a fixed problem as a relative one.

2. **"You see, my son, time here becomes space. / Du siehst, mein Sohn, zum Raum wird hier die Zeit."** — Gurnemanz, *Parsifal*, Wagner. Progress measured not in distance but in inner state; one stride can transport you. *Use it as* a closer on growth, mastery, or immersive design — the turn from physical to spiritual movement.

3. **Sankofa** (Twi, Ghana): "to go back and get" — the bird with its head turned backwards, feet facing forward, carrying an egg. "It is not wrong to go back for that which you have forgotten." — Retrieval as forward motion. *Use it to* preface anything on heritage, revival, learning from the past, or restoring lost craft.

4. **"Whom the gods would destroy, they would first put on the cover of BusinessWeek."** — Paul Krugman (riffing the classical "whom the gods would destroy they first make mad"). Public acclaim marks the top, not the floor. *Use it as* an aside on hype cycles, peak-signaling, contrarian timing.

5. **"To the mountainous Inca and their unadorned masonry, the stone was the point."** — Ornament as confession of doubt; the confident material needs no decoration. *Use it to* open or close on minimalism, materials, honest design, restraint.

## By function

### Openers

- **Etak (Micronesian Navigation).** "Etak refers to a method of mental navigation in which the navigator envisions themselves as stationary, while islands and stars move." It breaks a voyage into segments, each marked by a star's position. *Gloss:* movement reframed as relative — the world flows past a still observer. *Use it to* open on perspective, wayfinding, interface, or reframing.
- **Chronos and kairos** (Greek): *Chronos* = quantitative clock-time; *Kairos* = "the right moment, the opportune moment" — the archer's perfect opening to loose the arrow. *Gloss:* two kinds of time, measured vs. ripe. *Use it to* open on timing, opportunity, when-not-just-what.
- **"When you don't know where you're going, any road can get you there."** — Ross Scott. *Gloss:* without a destination, all paths are equal (and equally useless). *Use it to* open on aimlessness, strategy, the cost of no north star.
- **"There are decades where nothing happens, and there are weeks where decades happen."** — Vladimir Lenin. *Gloss:* history is lumpy; change clusters. *Use it to* open on inflection points, acceleration, AI moments.
- **Mise en place** (French): "everything in its place" — preparing and arranging all ingredients before cooking; "a state of mind," readiness as discipline. *Gloss:* order before action. *Use it to* open on preparation, process, craft discipline.
- **"We have Paleolithic emotions, medieval institutions, and God-like technology."** — attributed in notes to "Bill gates" (the line is E.O. Wilson's). *Gloss:* our power has outrun our wisdom and our structures. *Use it to* open on tech ethics, AI, mismatch of capability and maturity. *(Verify attribution — notes credit Gates; widely E.O. Wilson.)*
- **"Sprezzatura"** (Italian, Castiglione 1528): "studied carelessness" — making the meticulously planned look effortless, hiding the labor. *Gloss:* the art that conceals art. *Use it to* open on craft, design polish, the invisible work behind ease.
- **The Diderot Effect:** Diderot's beautiful new scarlet robe made all his old possessions look shabby — "no more coordination, no more unity, no more beauty" — so he replaced everything. *Gloss:* one upgrade cascades into spiraling consumption. *Use it to* open on consumerism, scope creep, the cost of a single nice thing.
- **Blind men and the elephant** — each touches one part and declares the whole. Heisenberg's moral: "what we observe is not nature in itself, but nature exposed to our method of questioning." *Gloss:* partial views mistaken for the whole. *Use it to* open on perspective, measurement, models vs. reality.
- **"Time here becomes space."** — Gurnemanz, *Parsifal*, Wagner (full text in Best of the box). Also works as opener for immersion/progress essays.

### Asides / injects

- **"The good ideas will survive."** — Quentin Tarantino. *Use as* an aside reassuring against idea-theft or premature polish.
- **"Don't worry about people stealing your ideas. If your ideas are any good, you'll have to ram them down people's throats."** — Howard Aiken. *Use as* a sharper inject on the same theme.
- **"Whom the gods would destroy, they would first put on the cover of BusinessWeek."** — Paul Krugman. Magazine-cover indicator: acclaim signals the peak. *Use as* an aside on hype, contrarian timing.
- **"You know it's time to get out when the shoe shine boys start giving you stock tips."** — Joe Kennedy, before the 1929 crash. *Use as* an aside on bubbles, mass enthusiasm as a sell signal.
- **Navy touchscreen / USS McCain:** the new touchscreen bridge system couldn't track >150 ships without malfunctioning; the Navy's fix was to tell sailors to delete tracked ships before hitting the limit. *Gloss:* a UX/engineering shortcut with lethal consequences. *Use as* an aside on bad interfaces, deferred-risk hacks, "just delete the warning."
- **"Schlimmbesserung"** (German): a solution to a problem that makes the problem worse. *Use as* an inject on counterproductive fixes.
- **"Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic."** — Arthur C. Clarke; plus Karl Schroeder's twist: "…indistinguishable from nature." *Use as* an aside on AI seeming magical, or on tech disappearing into the everyday.
- **"AI is to intelligence as fake plants are to plants."** — Ross Scott. *Use as* a deflating aside on AI hype.
- **Gimbal lock:** the rings of a gimbal are hierarchical; when two rings become parallel they lose the ability to move independently. *Gloss:* hidden coupling collapses your degrees of freedom. *Use as* an aside on dependency, lost flexibility, hierarchy traps.
- **The Jesus Nut:** the single nut holding a helicopter's rotor — one point of failure for the whole craft. *Use as* an aside on single points of failure.
- **"The glock was the first point and click interface."** — Dave, Tactical Hive. *Use as* a punchy aside on interface design / directness.
- **Cocktail party effect** — selective hearing; the brain isolates one voice in a crowd. *Use as* an aside on attention, signal/noise, filtering.
- **The doorway effect** (location-updating effect): walking through a doorway makes you forget — the brain treats it as an "event boundary" separating memory sets; worse under cognitive load. *Use as* an aside on context-switching, memory, environment shaping cognition.
- **Marmite strategy** — one or the other, love it or hate it. *Use as* an aside on polarizing positioning.
- **"You campaign in poetry but govern in prose."** — Mario Cuomo. *Use as* an aside on the gap between vision and execution.

### Section prefaces

- **Sankofa** (full text in Best of the box) — *preface* a section on heritage, revival, going back to recover the lost.
- **Shokunin** (Japanese): more than "craftsman/artisan" — implies "an attitude and social consciousness… a social obligation to work his/her best for the general welfare of the people." — Tasio Odate. *Preface* a section on craft, vocation, responsibility in work.
- **Rules vs. Standards:** "Rules are those legal commands which differentiate legal from illegal behavior in a simple and clear way. Standards, however, are general legal criteria which are unclear and fuzzy and require complicated judiciary decision making." *Preface* a section on policy, spec design, bright lines vs. judgment.
- **Gesamtkunstwerk** (German, Wagner 1849): "total work of art" — unifying all art forms into one immersive whole; "The separate arts reach their fullest unity in the drama, which alone brings all of them into mutual interaction." *Preface* a section on holistic design, integration, the unified experience.
- **Arete** (Greek): excellence/virtue — "the highest state of a thing, person, or quality… the fulfillment of their potential and inherent function." *Preface* a section on excellence, fulfilling a thing's purpose.
- **Tinkuy** (Quechua): "a meeting of opposing forces that complement each other… where different thoughts, interests, feelings and aspirations confront and converge," producing new ways of thinking. *Preface* a section on synthesis, productive conflict, dialectic.
- **Kairos** (see Openers) also prefaces a section specifically on timing/opportunity.
- **Mimesis** — Aristotle: "Imitation is a creative act." Modern architects delude themselves that they build only on principles, when really it's also direct intuitive imitation. *(tightened from notes)*. *Preface* a section on influence, originality, the myth of pure first-principles.
- **The angiosperm–primate coevolution hypothesis:** flowering plants and primates shaped each other — fruit drove forward-facing eyes, color vision, grasping hands. *Gloss:* two systems co-evolving, neither prime mover. *Preface* a section on coevolution, mutual shaping (tech & users, tools & makers).
- **Promethean / "We could be gods, too."** — John Vervaeke. *Preface* a section on ambition, the Promethean spirit, becoming-godlike via technology.

### Navigation & wayfinding

- **Etak** (Micronesian) — you are still; the islands and stars move. *(Best of the box)*
- **Insect pathfinding** — ants count steps (a pedometer) and read polarized skylight for direction; bees integrate "optic flow." Ants in landmark-rich terrain trust landmarks over their internal compass; in barren terrain they trust the compass. *Gloss:* navigation strategy adapts to the richness of the environment. *Use as* an aside on heuristics, dead-reckoning vs. landmarks, when to trust the map vs. the territory.
- **"When you don't know where you're going, any road can get you there."** — Ross Scott. *(Openers)*
- **"Take an afternoon off and wander without a destination… Follow that discomfort and see where it leads."** — Paul Millerd. *Use as* an opener on exploration, undirected discovery.
- **"Feeling for the stones as you cross the river."** — (Chinese: crossing the river by feeling the stones). *Use as* an aside on incrementalism, cautious progress.
- **"When a bird lands on a branch, does it trust its branch or its wings?"** — *Use as* a closer on self-reliance vs. external support.

### Hubris & decline

- **"Whom the gods would destroy, they would first put on the cover of BusinessWeek."** — Krugman. *(Asides)* — plus the related "Whom the gods would destroy, they first make mad."
- **"You know it's time to get out when the shoe shine boys start giving you stock tips."** — Joe Kennedy. *(Asides)*
- **The Hathaway Effect** — Berkshire Hathaway (BRK) shares jiggling with Anne Hathaway's movie releases; a coincidence treated as signal. *Use as* an aside on spurious correlation, pattern-finding gone wrong.
- **"Whom the gods would destroy / cover stories underperform"** — Krugman's fuller observation: tallest-building naming rights, magazine cover stars, and stadium-naming sponsors all underperform afterward. *Use as* an aside on peak-signaling.
- **"Fixed fortifications are a monument to the stupidity of man."** — Patton. *Use as* an aside on rigidity, the Maginot trap, betting on static defenses.
- **Elagabalus & Akhenaten** — both emperors tried to convert their empires from a pantheon to a single sun-god monotheism with themselves as deity/high priest; both failed. *(tightened)*. *Use as* an aside on overreach, forced top-down change.
- **The owl of Athena / Minerva** — symbol of wisdom and erudition (Hegel: it "spreads its wings only with the falling of the dusk" — wisdom comes only after the fact). *Use as* an opener/closer on hindsight, late understanding.
- **"You can be deceived by perfect symmetry."** — Joe Rogan (paraphrasing "within beauty there is the potential for cruelty"). *Use as* an aside on surface vs. substance.

### Knowing & truth (bonus theme)

- **Blind men and the elephant** + Heisenberg's "nature exposed to our method of questioning." *(Openers)*
- **"Mirror Flower, Water Moon" (鏡花水月)** — Chinese chengyu: something seen but not touchable, beautiful but unattainable; or something simple on its surface that reflects a deeper meaning. *Use as* a preface on illusion, the unattainable, surface and depth.
- **"Truth suffers from too much analysis."** — Ancient Fremen saying, Frank Herbert, *Dune Messiah*. *Use as* an aside on over-thinking, paralysis by analysis.
- **Žižek's "third pill"** — there is no "outside" of ideology; appearances themselves produce our lived reality, and we stay bound by the enjoyment an illusion gives even when we know it's an illusion. *Use as* a preface on ideology, design that shapes reality, knowing-yet-doing.
- **Halbbildung** (Adorno, 1959) — "half-education": consuming the signs of culture without the formative process. *Use as* an aside on shallow expertise, content vs. cultivation.
- **Truism vs. tautology** — a truism is obviously true and says nothing new; a tautology is true by its logical form. *Use as* a precise aside distinguishing empty statements.
- **CUI BONO?** — "Who benefits?" *Use as* a one-line investigative aside.
- **"Medicine must be sweet, and truth must be beautiful — this has been man's folly since the days of Adam."** — Chekhov. *Use as* a closer on our refusal of unpleasant truths.

### Chinese & idiom toolbox (bonus)

- **破釜沉舟 *pò fǔ chén zhōu*** — "break the pots and sink the ships": destroy your own means of retreat to force all-out effort (Xiang Yu). Western cousins: burn the boats, cross the Rubicon, point of no return. *Use as* an opener/aside on commitment.
- **磨杵成針 *mó chǔ chéng zhēn*** — "grind an iron bar down to a fine needle": persevere at a hard task.
- **守株待兔 *shǒu zhū dài tù*** — "guard a tree-stump waiting for rabbits": wait idly for a windfall.
- **紙上談兵 *zhǐ shàng tán bīng*** — "discuss military tactics on paper": theory useless in practice.
- **畫蛇添足 *huà shé tiān zú*** — "add feet when drawing a snake": ruin it by over-improving.
- **對牛彈琴 *duì niú tán qín*** — "play the guqin to a cow": know your audience.
- **"It's better to be a warrior in a garden than a gardener in war."** — attributed (notes say "Ancient Chinese proverb"). *Use as* an aside on preparedness.
- **"One can conquer the world from a saddle, but one cannot rule from it."** — traditional Chinese saying. *Use as* a closer on the difference between winning and governing.
- **Zhuangzi's village** — "a village where you only ever go so far as to hear the sounds of the next village"; and Zhuangzi's butcher: "I follow what is inherently so" (cutting along the natural grain). *Use as* a preface on contentment / working with the grain.
