The one assumption that breaks

The template rect is the blueprint's intent — a fixed {x,y,w,h} stored per skin. The painted art is a separate generative interpretation of that blueprint. The live overlay is positioned from the template rect and trusts it absolutely. That trust is the bug: the paint model is told "keep every well exactly where it is," but a generative model isn't pixel-faithful — it nudges, resizes, and merges wells. Nothing ever measures the final art to correct the overlay. So the overlay sits at the blueprint coord while the hardware sits somewhere else, and the gap you dragged into existence above is every misalignment you've been seeing.

Where do the slot numbers even come from?

Not one place — three pipelines feed the same template.json, none of which look at the painted output:

1 · Fixed constants

frog, bondi, burger… One hard-coded layout in pixels, identical on every body. Zero adaptation to the specific art.

wild_proc.py → layout()
bx=252,by=530,bw=88…

2 · Mask-fit

biomech, halo… Largest inscribed rectangle per torso band of the AI silhouette. Per-body, but fit to the blueprint mask, not the paint.

wild_sculpt.py → max_rect()

3 · Layout-first

maw, wmp… Hard-coded polar coords; the body is grown around the ring. Coords precede the art entirely.

layout_radial()/capsule()

All three share the fatal line at the top of the pipeline: "No detection, no repair, ever." The rect is where the well was drawn, never where it was painted.

Two ways to close the gap

Flip the fix control above between them and watch the misalignment go to zero from opposite ends:

Masked inpaint — repaint only the body; hand the model a mask that marks the wells as preserve (black) so it physically cannot move them. The wells come back bit-exact at the blueprint coords. Drift can't happen → the overlay still fits. Crucially, the shared template stays valid across every skin: same generator emits blueprint and mask, zero per-skin re-measurement. (fal flux-pro/v1/fill or qwen-image-edit/inpaint, mask = body-white / wells-black.) The one cost is a crisp recessed seam at the well edge — on skeuomorphic hardware that reads as a machined bezel, not a defect.

Detect & snap — keep the free paint model, then run a CV / vision pass on the final frame to find where each well actually landed and rewrite the overlay coords to match. The art looks natural, but every skin must be re-measured and the overlay coords become per-skin — you lose the shared-template reuse. Accuracy is only as good as the detector (±several px for a vision-model bbox).

Verdict from the research pass: masked inpaint is the structural fix (exact by construction, keeps the shared template); detect-and-snap is the rescue path for art you've already generated, or a validator that the inpaint didn't bleed.
